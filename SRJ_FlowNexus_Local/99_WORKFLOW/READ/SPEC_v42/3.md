## 3. Entry workflow

### 3.0 Trading hours

Two windows only, Eastern Time: **London 02:00–05:00** and **New York AM 07:00–12:00**. Empirically confirmed in the broker frame as server **09:00–12:00** and **14:00–19:00** EET — eight hours a day, 33.3% of bars. Outside them, no evaluation of any kind.

### 3.1 Trigger

A POI retest fires (§2, row 1) on one of the twelve lines, inside a window. **The freshness nuance applies here** — this is one of the two places it survives.

### 3.2 Step 1 — regime classification

- **Trend-following:** retest direction agrees with the HTF aggregate (4H/1H/15m, simple majority, no timeframe weighted).
- **Mean-reversion:** justified by a **fresh** sweep of the immediately prior session's liquidity. Freshness's second surviving site.

Both may hold; no precedence, no differential treatment. Neither → the candidate does not advance.

**Measured, and it changes how the whole funnel should be read:** the HTF majority is the highest-volume filter in the system. On 1212 evaluations `trendOk` was false on **963 — 79%.** Mean-reversion classified on 134 and behaves exactly as written.

### 3.3 Step 2 — LTF structure alignment

The 5-minute bias-panel state must agree with the classified direction. Only the LTF's object-level state matters; the HTF contributes direction only.

**Live, never latched.** There is no neutral 5-minute bias. Re-checked every bar from zone-wait through gate-check; a flip against the locked direction kills the candidate. Post-entry it is ignored (§5.6).

### 3.4 Step 3 — continuous structural invalidation, pre-confirmation

Three flags: the in-bias order block's validity, the in-bias FVG's validity, and the validation of an opposing FVG. **Two or more adverse invalidates the setup.**

This replaces v3's "any single failure, at any point, invalidates the whole setup instantly," which was wrong. Operator, verbatim: *"The combinations to invalidate a setup before the actual bias flip is either OB+FVG invalidation or in bias FVG invalidation + opp FVG validation… if two out of three conditions have been met then the setup is also invalidated."*

Two load-bearing boundaries:

- **The rule is explicitly pre-confirmation.** *"This criteria is before the trade confirmed, if later the structure is flipped after the confirmation entry, i still hold the trade."* It must not run after the confirming close.
- **Post-confirmation cancellation requires all three flags**, which is the same set as a weak bias flip. So the post-confirmation cancellation condition collapses to "the bias flipped."

**Staleness and structural renewal never invalidate.** A new same-direction OB or FVG elsewhere is normal continuation. A same-direction higher-tier POI touch mid-sequence upgrades the anchor tier silently.

**Open, and the largest single miss cause.** The three flags are *global* in-bias flags; the entry zone comes from a different selector. This section's own wording is "the order block behind the current entry zone." Whether the operator watches the specific order block behind his zone, or any in-bias order block anywhere, is unresolved. Three journalled trades were killed by these flags on bars where the operator was in the trade.

### 3.5 Step 4 — the qualifying retracement zone

One of:

- an XOB that is **relevant and in play**; or
- an **FVG** that formed within the current, still-open expansion leg of a valid XOB, including a promoted one. A leg begins with an XOB followed by FVG(s) and ends on either a new same-direction OB+FVG or a bias flip. A standalone FVG outside any open leg never qualifies.

**In play, for an XOB.** Its zone, projected rightward from formation, has been penetrated by a bar's range or by a confirmed protective-side swing **at any point within the current structural leg**, and the XOB has not been invalidated.

> **There is no recency requirement and no bar-count limit.** v3's *"being retested by the current, freshest structure"* is withdrawn for XOB in-play. The one verified operator zone was in play via a swing low approximately **eighty bars** before entry, on an XOB **ten hours** old. Any implementation restricting in-play to the most recent one or two swings contradicts this rule and **cannot accept the only verified operator zone** — see §9.10.

**In play, for an FVG.** The FVG has not been filled or invalidated, and it lies within the current open leg. An FVG has no projection, so its price range is fixed at formation.

**No precedence between XOB and FVG.** Validity is the only gate. Any leg FVG qualifies, not only the most extreme. **Size is never a criterion** — a one-point three-candle gap is a valid imbalance.

**No protective-side filter on the zone.** A structurally valid zone above price for a LONG is still a candidate zone. The *stop reference* carries a side test (§3.7); different object.

**Depth is not an admission criterion**, and the leg-opening FVG is included.

#### 3.5.1 Relevance must precede retracement

**A zone that becomes relevant only after price has already retraced into it is not tradeable.** Operator: *"that means the OB has not in play or influence the movement yet."*

Required order: **relevance → retracement or opposing candle → confirmation candle.**

- **If the order block is not yet promoted at the confirmation candle, that entry is invalid.** Not deferred — invalid, at that candle.
- **If promoted later, a fresh retracement and a fresh confirmation are required.** *"then i need another retracement candle or the opposite candle and then another confirmation candle."*

This **overturns v3 Step 5's "no re-arm mechanic."** A promotion event clears any accumulated touch.

> **Honesty limit.** No export records when an order block was promoted, so this bound cannot be implemented **or verified**. Two observed instances of adopt-and-inherit — a zone replaced on the arming bar, inheriting an earlier touch — produced one signal on a session the operator explicitly rejected, and one signal on a zone the operator **did not trade**. **The pattern has no validated instance and one refuted instance.**
>
> It is **not** primarily a selector defect. Even handed the operator's exact zone, the orchestrator's in-play test would have rejected it on every bar, because that test consults only the two most recent protective-side swings (§9.10). The selector's late switch is downstream of a test that could not accept the correct zone. Widening the in-play window may remove the pattern's cause without any relevance bound at all.

### 3.6 Step 5 — the retracement and the confirmation candle

A **retracement candle** (equivalently, an **opposing candle**) closes against the trade direction. Entry fires on the close of the **next candle that closes in the trade direction**. Any nonzero body counts. No requirement to close beyond the retracement candle's extreme or the zone boundary. Long wicks are fine. Only a true doji is rejected.

**The opposing candle's zone interaction differs by zone type, and this is the rule, not an implementation detail:**

| Zone source | Opposing candle must touch the zone? |
|---|---|
| **XOB** | **Not required.** A touch is **permitted** and is **never disqualifying**. The XOB's in-play status is established separately per §3.5, and that is what ties the opposing candle to the zone |
| **FVG** | **Required.** An FVG cannot be projected, so the retracement must touch or retrace to the FVG level, and the touch must be attributed to **that specific opposing candle** |

> **★ This is a relaxation, not a reversal. ★** An implementation must accept an XOB-sourced candidate whose opposing candle touches the zone **and** one whose opposing candle does not. Any test that rejects either case is stricter than this rule. See §10 for the permission vocabulary this turns on.
>
> **The one verified case is a touching instance** — the 09:10 opposing candle penetrated the XOB at 1.15835 inside 1.15805–1.15843. So 08.17 demonstrates the optional touch occurring, not its absence. **The non-touching case is permitted by ruling and has no observed instance.**

For an FVG the touch must be attributed to that opposing candle, not to some other bar. Operator: *"fresh can't be done with the other candle except the specific retracement candle or the opposing candle before confirmation pre entry."*

> Marked as inference: I read "the opposing candle before confirmation" as the candle constituting the retracement wherever it sits within the current structural leg, **not** the bar immediately adjacent to the confirmation candle. Under the carried wide reading that candle may be older than the bar on which a candidate first becomes eligible. If adjacency is in fact required, that is a tightening and must be stated.

**This is a condition set, not a sequence.** The same bar may be simultaneously the POI retest and the confirmation candle.

**The entry is not required to be inside the zone.** The zone must be in play; the confirming close may be arbitrarily far from it. The one measured case entered **101 points above its zone's high**, in play via a swing that also carried the stop. Any implementation requiring the confirming or retracing bar to overlap an XOB is stricter than this rule.

No bar-count limit. Subject to §3.5.1's re-arm on promotion, additional touches are irrelevant.

### 3.7 Step 6 — the 1R admission gate

**Pre-entry:** the nearest valid take-profit target in the trade direction must imply at least 1R, or the setup is not taken. Targets are the nearest of: nearest relevant session-liquidity level, the same-tier VWAP/POC on the opposite side, or a higher-tier VWAP/POC. **VWAP and POC on one anchor are the same tier.** Evaluated continuously — targets move.

A target is **valid** unless (a) already swept as session liquidity, or (b) closed over. A target lying inside the entry zone is not a target.

**1R is an admission criterion only. It has no role in management.** Post-entry the target follows the nearest valid POI wherever it goes, **with no floor**, and the trade is held. Operator: *"I still hold the trade that is why there are less than 1R gain even though upon entry I actually target more than 1R."*

**The reward/risk reference is the confirming close, latched.** Price moving away afterwards moves nothing and cancels nothing. Where a later condition validates at a better level, R is recomputed and **the reference producing the higher valid R is selected** — better R, not better price. The two diverge: one measured case improved 71 points in the trade's favour while R fell from 1.01 to 0.06.

**Ordering is part of the rule.** R is computed once **every** condition has validated. A candidate must not fail the gate before a pending divergence has had the opportunity to supply the permitted better reference. Measured: **29 of 33 hard RR failures fired while divergence was still unlatched — 88%.**

**Stop-loss reference distance, two branches:**

| Branch | Condition | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| One swing | a valid order block **with** an imbalance marks the structural extreme | one swing from that order block's swing high/low |
| Two swings | **strong-signal scenario with no imbalance** — the opposite-side order block invalidated, no FVG present | two swings, plain candle swings, no order block required |

A *swing* is the three-candle pattern where the middle candle is the extreme. **The stop is a swing high or low, not an order-block extreme.** Operator: *"even though it is only a valid swing pattern… and NOT tied to an order block, and even though it is not the most structurally extreme, I still consider or place my stop loss on that swing."*

The reference must lie on the **protective side** of the entry. An unusable candidate is skipped and the walk continues to the next valid swing further back; it never aborts where a valid swing exists.

**No minimum stop distance.** *"I am okay with the 12-point stop or however small it is as long as it is valid."*

**The stop may lie inside the entry zone.** Measured: a stop at 1.15835 inside a zone spanning 1.15805–1.15843, with 109 points of risk from an entry at 1.15944. A guard excluding in-zone stop references is premised on the entry sitting *at* the zone and is invalid when it does not. **The correct test is the stop's side relative to the entry, never its containment.**

**Branch selection is the two-OB state plus imbalance presence** — the operator-facing `2xOB` on his bias panel — **not** the order-block validity flag.

> **Confirmed defect with a named instance.** On the one trade checkable against ground truth, the operator's setup was strong-signal / no-imbalance and his stop was the two-swing reference at 1.15835. The orchestrator emitted `sl_mode=1-swing` with `sl_ref=1.15870`, selected on `obValid=1` alone with the imbalance term never consulted — 35 points away, and diagnostics show `obStruct == obSwing == zoneLo`, meaning the emitted reference was the **adopted zone's lower boundary rather than a swing at all**. The nearest actual swing found was 1.15907. Neither figure is the operator's.
>
> Qualification: the risk denominators were nearly identical (109 vs 114 points), so on this instance the branch error cost five points of risk while a three-bar entry delay cost forty. And whether a two-swing walk would reach 1.15835 at all is unverified — the swing series may not contain it.

### 3.8 Step 7 — order-flow divergence

A qualifying, direction-matched divergence must occur **at least once**, at any point in the sequence — before, at, or after the confirming candle. No bar-count limit. Once observed it counts permanently and is never re-invalidated by a later piercing or an opposing divergence. Plain directional agreement without a qualifying pattern is never a substitute. No partial-credit setups anywhere.

The verdict carries four states: regular and hidden, bullish and bearish. The operator's 1/2/3/4 numbering is a presentation layer over the same four.
