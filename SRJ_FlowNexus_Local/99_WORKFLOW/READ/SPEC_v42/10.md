## 10. Permission vocabulary

Three of this document's rules turn on the difference between a requirement, a permission, and a prohibition, and one earlier revision conflated the second with the third. The distinction is stated once here and applies throughout.

| Phrasing | Implementation | Rejects when |
|---|---|---|
| **Required** / must | test the condition, reject on failure | the condition is absent |
| **Permitted** / not required / need not | **accept either way**; the condition is informational at most | never — a permission cannot cause a rejection |
| **Prohibited** / must not | test the condition, reject on presence | the condition is present |

**A permission is not a prohibition.** "The opposing candle need not touch the XOB" means a touching candle and a non-touching candle are both admissible. It does **not** mean a touch disqualifies, and no ruling in this document creates such a prohibition.

Where this matters:

| Rule | Strength | Common misreading |
|---|---|---|
| §3.6 XOB opposing-candle touch | **permitted** | that a touch is disqualifying, or that only non-touching candles qualify |
| §3.6 entry inside the zone | **permitted** | that entry must be inside the zone (v3's reading) |
| §3.7 stop inside the entry zone | **permitted** | that an in-zone stop is invalid (the current guard's reading) |
| §3.6 FVG opposing-candle touch | **required** | — |
| §3.5.1 relevance before retracement | **required** | — |
